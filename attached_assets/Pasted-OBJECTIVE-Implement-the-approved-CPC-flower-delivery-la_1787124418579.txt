OBJECTIVE

Implement the approved CPC flower-delivery landing-page redesign for Beirut, then roll the same conversion-focused structure out to Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

REFERENCE DIRECTION

Use the approved CEO mockup and the current Beirut flower-delivery landing page as the visual reference. Preserve Presentail’s premium teal-and-ivory design system, serif headings, product photography style, and existing market-specific commerce logic.

SCOPE

1. Compress the header and hero
- Reduce the desktop header to approximately 68–72 px.
- Create an appropriately compact mobile header.
- Reduce the desktop hero to approximately 320–350 px so products appear substantially earlier.
- Keep the Presentail wordmark, search, account, and cart controls.
- Preserve strong text contrast over the hero image.
- Optimize the hero image responsively so page speed does not regress.

2. Use one primary hero buying path
- Primary CTA text: “Shop flowers available today”
- CTA should scroll or navigate directly to the Flowers inventory shown on the page.
- Remove the second hero button.
- Add this secondary text link below the primary CTA: “Need help choosing? Chat with a support agent”
- Route the link into the existing support-agent flow appropriate to each market.

3. Localize hero content by market
- Beirut: “Same-day flower delivery in Beirut”
- Dubai: “Same-day flower delivery in Dubai”
- Abu Dhabi: “Same-day flower delivery in Abu Dhabi”
- Localize the status pill, opening status, cutoff time, delivery-speed claim, currency, and supporting copy using the real rules for each city.
- Do not copy Beirut’s 10 PM cutoff or 90-minute promise into Dubai or Abu Dhabi unless operations confirms that they apply.
- Preserve any campaign parameters, including UTM values and GCLID, through the shopping journey.

4. Show Flowers first
- The first merchandise section must be titled “Flowers”.
- Subtitle: “Fresh arrangements ready to deliver today”
- Show only flower bouquets and floral arrangements in this first section.
- Exclude cakes, chocolates, balloons, gift boxes, and other non-flower items.
- Prioritize in-stock, same-day-eligible best sellers for the visitor’s selected market.
- Include a “View all flowers” link.

5. Add Luxury Arrangements second
- The second merchandise section must be titled “Luxury Arrangements”.
- Subtitle: “Statement designs for unforgettable moments”
- Show premium, high-value floral arrangements only.
- Exclude standalone cakes, chocolates, balloons, and other non-flower gifts.
- Use the correct live inventory, availability, pricing, and currency for Beirut, Dubai, and Abu Dhabi.
- Include a “View all arrangements” link.

MARKETS / PAGES

- Beirut flower-delivery CPC landing page
- Dubai flower-delivery CPC landing page
- Abu Dhabi flower-delivery CPC landing page

Reuse components where practical, but keep all inventory queries, cutoff logic, delivery messaging, support routing, currency, and URLs market-aware.

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

- Compact header and hero are live on all three market pages.
- Each hero contains exactly one button: “Shop flowers available today”.
- The support-agent option is a text link, not a competing button.
- Flowers is the first product section and contains no non-flower gifts.
- Luxury Arrangements is the second product section and contains premium floral products only.
- Beirut, Dubai, and Abu Dhabi show correct local inventory, currency, delivery rules, cutoff times, and support routing.
- Layout is QA’d on common desktop and mobile breakpoints.
- Primary CTA, support link, product click, add-to-cart, checkout-start, and purchase tracking continue to fire correctly.
- UTM and GCLID attribution are preserved.
- Hero and product images are responsive, correctly sized, and do not cause a material Core Web Vitals regression.
- Staging is reviewed before production rollout.
- After launch, verify each market page and document any market-specific exceptions or follow-up work.

DELIVERABLE

Production rollout across Beirut, Dubai, and Abu Dhabi, with staging links and a short QA summary added to this task before release.
