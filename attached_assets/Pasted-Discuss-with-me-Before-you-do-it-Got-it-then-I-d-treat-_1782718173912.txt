Got it — then I’d treat this as a general account login page, not a checkout step.
In that case, I’d revise the recommendations like this:
What I’d improve
1. Change the title to be more welcoming
Instead of:
Login or Create Account
Use:
Welcome to Presentail
Subtitle:
Sign in or create an account to manage your orders, addresses, and reminders.
This makes the page feel less transactional.
2. Clarify that account creation is automatic
“Login or Create Account” can make users wonder which path they are on. A better approach:
Enter your email to continue
“We’ll sign you in or help you create an account.”
This feels smoother and less intimidating.
3. Make the primary CTA stronger
The current Continue button looks muted, almost disabled.
Use:
Continue with Email
If disabled until email is entered, that is fine, but once valid input is entered it should become strong dark teal.
4. Reorder social login options
For a general login page, Apple and Google can be shown first because they are faster:
Continue with Apple
Continue with Google
Then a divider:
or continue with email
Email field
Continue with Email
This reduces friction for returning users.
5. Add useful account benefits
Since this is not checkout, explain why logging in is worth it:
“Access past orders, saved addresses, upcoming reminders, and faster checkout.”
This gives the page a purpose beyond authentication.
6. Reduce header complexity
The current header has menu, search, logo, account, cart, city, language. For login, I’d simplify slightly:
Keep logo centered
Keep language/location if important
Remove or de-emphasize account icon, since the user is already on login
Keep cart icon only if users commonly log in from shopping
7. Improve the top gray strip
The top strip with city/language feels detached and a little faded. I’d either:
Make it cleaner and more intentional, or
Move Beirut / EN into the main header row
Right now it looks like a partially hidden banner.
8. Push the footer lower
The footer appears too soon and is partially covered by Safari. On login pages, it should not compete with the form. Either remove it from the initial mobile viewport or make the login page vertically centered with footer below the fold.
9. Add “Need help?” or support link
Useful for account issues:
Having trouble signing in? Contact us
This is especially helpful if login uses OTP, email code, or social auth.
10. Add privacy reassurance
Small text under the form:
“We’ll never share your email. We’ll use it for your account and order updates.”
This reduces hesitation.
