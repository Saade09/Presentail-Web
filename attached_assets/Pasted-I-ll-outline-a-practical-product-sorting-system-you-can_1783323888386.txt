I’ll outline a practical product-sorting system you can ship in stages: a first score formula, key ranking signals, business and quality factors, then personalization and testing as data grows.

Thought for 1m 35s

Here’s a practical product-sorting algorithm you can build for your website.

The core idea: filter first, then rank, then let users override with explicit sort options. Product lists, category pages, filters, and sorting are central to whether shoppers can actually find products, and Baymard’s ecommerce UX research shows many sites still underperform badly on product-list usability. Nielsen Norman Group similarly treats search results, product listing pages, and category pages as key “routing pages” that help shoppers decide what to buy.

1. Use this structure

For every product request, run this pipeline:

1. Apply hard filters
2. Calculate product score
3. Apply merchandising rules
4. Sort products
5. Log impressions, clicks, add-to-carts, and purchases
6. Improve weights over time
2. Hard filters first

Before scoring, remove products that should not appear.

Examples:

Exclude if:
- product is out of stock
- product is not available in the shopper’s country
- product is hidden, discontinued, or unpublished
- product does not match selected filters
- product violates category constraints

This prevents a high-scoring but unavailable item from appearing near the top.

3. Default “Recommended” ranking formula

For your default sort, use a weighted score:

recommended_score =
  0.25 * relevance_score
+ 0.20 * popularity_score
+ 0.15 * conversion_score
+ 0.10 * rating_score
+ 0.10 * margin_score
+ 0.08 * inventory_score
+ 0.07 * freshness_score
+ 0.05 * delivery_score
- 0.10 * return_rate_penalty

Each score should be normalized from 0 to 1.

For category pages, relevance_score means “how well this product belongs in this category.”
For search results, relevance_score means “how well this product matches the user’s query.” Search platforms commonly separate matching from ranking; Algolia, for example, describes custom ranking as a way to influence order using attributes like popularity, rating, sales, or release date after relevance criteria are applied.

4. Define each signal
relevance_score

For category pages:

1.0 = primary category match
0.7 = related subcategory match
0.4 = weak related category match
0.0 = no match

For search:

relevance_score =
  title_match * 0.45
+ brand_match * 0.20
+ category_match * 0.20
+ attribute_match * 0.15

Example: a search for “black leather wallet” should heavily reward products with “wallet” in the title, “black” as a color attribute, and “leather” as a material attribute.

popularity_score

Use recent demand, not all-time demand.

popularity_score = normalize(
  views_7d * 0.1
+ clicks_7d * 0.2
+ add_to_carts_30d * 0.3
+ purchases_30d * 0.4
)

This keeps the ranking fresh.

conversion_score
conversion_score = purchases / product_page_views

But protect against low sample sizes:

adjusted_conversion =
  (purchases + category_avg_conversion * 50)
  / (views + 50)

This avoids ranking a product first just because it had 1 sale from 2 views.

rating_score

Do not use raw average rating alone. A product with 5.0 stars from 2 reviews should not outrank a product with 4.7 stars from 800 reviews.

Use a Bayesian-style rating:

rating_score =
  (review_count / (review_count + 25)) * product_rating
+ (25 / (review_count + 25)) * category_avg_rating

Then normalize to 0–1.

margin_score

Use margin carefully. It should influence ranking, not dominate it.

margin_score = normalize(gross_margin_percentage)

Cap its effect so you do not bury better customer choices.

inventory_score

Reward products that are in stock and healthy.

inventory_score =
  1.0 if inventory is healthy
  0.6 if low stock
  0.2 if very low stock
  0.0 if out of stock

You may want the opposite for clearance products: if your goal is liquidation, low stock or aged inventory can get a controlled boost.

freshness_score

Useful for fashion, electronics, beauty, seasonal goods, media, and trend-driven categories.

freshness_score = exp(-days_since_launch / 45)

This gives newer products a boost that fades over time.

delivery_score
delivery_score =
  1.0 for same-day / next-day delivery
  0.7 for 2–3 days
  0.4 for 4–7 days
  0.1 for slow or uncertain delivery
return_rate_penalty

Penalize products that create bad customer outcomes.

return_rate_penalty = normalize(product_return_rate - category_avg_return_rate)

Also consider penalties for:

- high cancellation rate
- high defect rate
- low review sentiment
- many customer support complaints
5. Add business rules, but keep them bounded

After scoring, you can apply merchandising rules.

Good examples:

- Pin sponsored product to position 3, not position 1
- Boost private-label items by up to 5%
- Boost seasonal campaign products by up to 10%
- Demote products with high return rates
- Limit one brand to max 3 products in the first 12 results
- Avoid showing near-duplicate variants repeatedly

Important: do not let manual rules completely override relevance. Shoppers notice when the first results are clearly not what they wanted.

Baymard specifically warns that product variations shown separately can clutter product lists and make it harder for users to understand the actual product range, so it is often better to combine variants into one list item with visible swatches or options.

6. Support explicit sort options

Your default should be Recommended, but users should be able to choose alternatives.

Recommended sort options:

Recommended
Best Sellers
Newest
Price: Low to High
Price: High to Low
Highest Rated
Most Reviewed
Discount
Fastest Delivery

When the user chooses an explicit sort, respect it.

For example:

Price: Low to High

should sort by price, not by your recommended score. You can still use the recommended score as a tie-breaker.

sort by price ascending, then recommended_score descending
7. TypeScript example

Here is a simple implementation you can adapt.

type Product = {
  id: string;
  title: string;
  brand: string;
  categoryIds: string[];
  price: number;
  grossMarginPct: number;
  rating: number; // 0 to 5
  reviewCount: number;
  inventoryCount: number;
  isPublished: boolean;
  isDiscontinued: boolean;
  availableCountries: string[];
  views7d: number;
  clicks7d: number;
  addToCarts30d: number;
  purchases30d: number;
  pageViews30d: number;
  returnRate: number;
  launchedAt: string;
  deliveryDays: number;
};

type SortMode =
  | "recommended"
  | "price_asc"
  | "price_desc"
  | "newest"
  | "best_sellers"
  | "highest_rated";

type RankingContext = {
  country: string;
  categoryId?: string;
  query?: string;
  categoryAvgRating: number;
  categoryAvgConversion: number;
  categoryAvgReturnRate: number;
  maxPopularityRaw: number;
  maxMarginPct: number;
};

function clamp01(value: number): number {
  return Math.max(0, Math.min(1, value));
}

function safeDivide(numerator: number, denominator: number): number {
  return denominator === 0 ? 0 : numerator / denominator;
}

function daysSince(dateString: string): number {
  const launched = new Date(dateString).getTime();
  const now = Date.now();
  return Math.max(0, (now - launched) / (1000 * 60 * 60 * 24));
}

function isEligible(product: Product, ctx: RankingContext): boolean {
  if (!product.isPublished) return false;
  if (product.isDiscontinued) return false;
  if (product.inventoryCount <= 0) return false;
  if (!product.availableCountries.includes(ctx.country)) return false;

  if (ctx.categoryId && !product.categoryIds.includes(ctx.categoryId)) {
    return false;
  }

  return true;
}

function relevanceScore(product: Product, ctx: RankingContext): number {
  if (!ctx.query) {
    return ctx.categoryId && product.categoryIds.includes(ctx.categoryId) ? 1 : 0.5;
  }

  const q = ctx.query.toLowerCase();
  const title = product.title.toLowerCase();
  const brand = product.brand.toLowerCase();

  let score = 0;

  if (title.includes(q)) score += 0.6;
  if (brand.includes(q)) score += 0.2;

  const queryTokens = q.split(/\s+/).filter(Boolean);
  const titleTokenMatches = queryTokens.filter(token => title.includes(token)).length;
  score += 0.2 * safeDivide(titleTokenMatches, queryTokens.length);

  return clamp01(score);
}

function popularityScore(product: Product, ctx: RankingContext): number {
  const raw =
    product.views7d * 0.1 +
    product.clicks7d * 0.2 +
    product.addToCarts30d * 0.3 +
    product.purchases30d * 0.4;

  return clamp01(safeDivide(raw, ctx.maxPopularityRaw));
}

function conversionScore(product: Product, ctx: RankingContext): number {
  const smoothingViews = 50;
  const adjusted =
    (product.purchases30d + ctx.categoryAvgConversion * smoothingViews) /
    (product.pageViews30d + smoothingViews);

  // Example cap: 20% conversion becomes max score.
  return clamp01(adjusted / 0.2);
}

function ratingScore(product: Product, ctx: RankingContext): number {
  const priorWeight = 25;

  const adjustedRating =
    (product.reviewCount / (product.reviewCount + priorWeight)) * product.rating +
    (priorWeight / (product.reviewCount + priorWeight)) * ctx.categoryAvgRating;

  return clamp01(adjustedRating / 5);
}

function inventoryScore(product: Product): number {
  if (product.inventoryCount > 20) return 1.0;
  if (product.inventoryCount > 5) return 0.6;
  if (product.inventoryCount > 0) return 0.2;
  return 0;
}

function freshnessScore(product: Product): number {
  return Math.exp(-daysSince(product.launchedAt) / 45);
}

function deliveryScore(product: Product): number {
  if (product.deliveryDays <= 1) return 1.0;
  if (product.deliveryDays <= 3) return 0.7;
  if (product.deliveryDays <= 7) return 0.4;
  return 0.1;
}

function marginScore(product: Product, ctx: RankingContext): number {
  return clamp01(safeDivide(product.grossMarginPct, ctx.maxMarginPct));
}

function returnRatePenalty(product: Product, ctx: RankingContext): number {
  const excessReturnRate = product.returnRate - ctx.categoryAvgReturnRate;

  if (excessReturnRate <= 0) return 0;

  // Example: 30 percentage points above category average = max penalty.
  return clamp01(excessReturnRate / 0.3);
}

function recommendedScore(product: Product, ctx: RankingContext): number {
  return (
    0.25 * relevanceScore(product, ctx) +
    0.20 * popularityScore(product, ctx) +
    0.15 * conversionScore(product, ctx) +
    0.10 * ratingScore(product, ctx) +
    0.10 * marginScore(product, ctx) +
    0.08 * inventoryScore(product) +
    0.07 * freshnessScore(product) +
    0.05 * deliveryScore(product) -
    0.10 * returnRatePenalty(product, ctx)
  );
}

function sortProducts(
  products: Product[],
  ctx: RankingContext,
  sortMode: SortMode = "recommended"
): Product[] {
  const eligibleProducts = products.filter(product => isEligible(product, ctx));

  return eligibleProducts.sort((a, b) => {
    const scoreA = recommendedScore(a, ctx);
    const scoreB = recommendedScore(b, ctx);

    switch (sortMode) {
      case "price_asc":
        return a.price - b.price || scoreB - scoreA;

      case "price_desc":
        return b.price - a.price || scoreB - scoreA;

      case "newest":
        return new Date(b.launchedAt).getTime() - new Date(a.launchedAt).getTime() || scoreB - scoreA;

      case "best_sellers":
        return b.purchases30d - a.purchases30d || scoreB - scoreA;

      case "highest_rated":
        return ratingScore(b, ctx) - ratingScore(a, ctx) || scoreB - scoreA;

      case "recommended":
      default:
        return scoreB - scoreA;
    }
  });
}
8. Start with these weights

For a general ecommerce store:

Relevance:       25%
Popularity:      20%
Conversion:      15%
Rating:          10%
Margin:          10%
Inventory:        8%
Freshness:        7%
Delivery speed:   5%
Return penalty: -10%

For a fashion store:

Increase freshness and visual/category relevance.
Lower rating weight if reviews are sparse.

For electronics:

Increase rating, review count, delivery speed, and availability.
Lower freshness unless new models matter a lot.

For groceries or consumables:

Increase availability, delivery speed, repeat purchases, and price competitiveness.
Lower freshness.

For marketplace products:

Add seller quality, cancellation rate, shipping reliability, and dispute rate.

10. Improve it in phases
Phase 1: Rules-based ranking

Build the formula above. This is fast, explainable, and good enough for most early ecommerce sites.

Phase 2: A/B testing

Test different weights:

Version A: higher popularity
Version B: higher conversion
Version C: higher margin
Version D: higher freshness

Pick winners by gross profit per visitor, conversion, and return-adjusted revenue.

Phase 3: Personalization

Once you have enough traffic, add user-level signals:

preferred brands
preferred price range
preferred sizes
previous purchases
browsing history
wishlist items
location
delivery preference

Then modify the score:

final_score =
  recommended_score * 0.85
+ personalization_score * 0.15

Start small. Personalization should improve relevance without creating a strange or overly narrow product list.

11. SEO and product data

Sorting is mostly an on-site UX/conversion issue, but product data quality also matters for search engines and external shopping surfaces. Google’s ecommerce product structured-data documentation says product pages can expose price, availability, review ratings, shipping, returns, and variant information to help Google understand and display product information.

So your product data model should include:

title
brand
category
price
sale price
availability
rating
review count
shipping details
return policy
product variants
GTIN / SKU / MPN where applicable
My recommended default

Use this as your first production version:

Default sort: Recommended

1. Remove unavailable products
2. Match category/search intent
3. Rank by:
   - relevance
   - popularity
   - conversion rate
   - rating quality
   - inventory health
   - freshness
   - delivery speed
   - margin
   - return-rate penalty
4. Add bounded merchandising boosts
5. Respect user-selected sort options
6. A/B test weights monthly

This gives you a sorting system that balances customer relevance, sales performance, and business profitability without making the site feel manipulated.
