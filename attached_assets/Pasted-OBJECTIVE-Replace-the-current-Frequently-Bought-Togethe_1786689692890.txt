OBJECTIVE

Replace the current “Frequently Bought Together” section on the product detail page with a conversion-focused “Complete Your Gift” upsell module. The module must show one personalized product from each of four fixed categories, in this exact order:

1. Chocolate
2. Cake
3. Balloon
4. Stuffed animal

The category order remains predictable for the customer. The recommendation system ranks eligible products within each category and chooses the best product to display.

BUSINESS GOALS

- Increase add-on attach rate and average order value without reducing the primary bouquet conversion rate.
- Make the upsell interaction clear, fast, and trustworthy.
- Gather clean impression, selection, cart, purchase, margin, cancellation, and refund data for future ranking improvements.
- Prevent irrelevant, unavailable, or operationally risky products from being recommended.

CUSTOMER-FACING COPY

Heading: Complete your gift
Supporting copy: Add a thoughtful extra to your bouquet.
Initial card button: + Add
Selected card button: ✓ Added
Initial summary example: 1 item · AED 190
Selected summary example: 3 items · AED 280
Initial primary CTA: Add bouquet to cart
Dynamic primary CTA: Add {item_count} items to cart

MODULE STRUCTURE

- Keep the primary bouquet visually separate and locked as “Your bouquet.”
- Show the bouquet thumbnail, product name, and current price.
- The bouquet is not selectable or removable inside this module.
- Show up to four add-on cards to the right of the bouquet.
- Add-on slots must be ordered Chocolate, Cake, Balloon, Stuffed animal.
- Display no more than one product per category.
- Each add-on card must contain product image, product name, incremental AED price, and a clear + Add control.
- Use “AED 45,” not an unfamiliar currency glyph.
- Do not show radio buttons.
- Do not show quantity controls before an item is selected.
- Use accessible color contrast and a clear selected state.
- Maintain the current Presentail visual system: dark teal, white/warm-white surfaces, subtle borders, rounded cards, and minimal shadow.

ELIGIBILITY FILTERS — APPLY BEFORE RANKING

A product must not enter the candidate set unless all applicable conditions pass:

- Active and purchasable SKU/variant.
- In stock with sufficient sellable inventory.
- Deliverable to the selected country, city, and delivery zone.
- Available for the selected delivery date and time window.
- Compatible with the fulfillment method and vendor fulfilling the bouquet.
- Meets relevant lead-time and cutoff requirements.
- Price and currency are valid for the active storefront.
- Not already present as an equivalent item in the current cart unless multiples are intentionally allowed.
- Not suppressed by merchandising or operational rules.
- Cake products must account for available flavor/size variants, handling, refrigeration, lead time, and allergen metadata where applicable.
- Exclude products with unacceptable recent cancellation, substitution, refund, damage, or fulfillment-failure rates.
- Do not return duplicate products across slots.

RANKING BEHAVIOR

Rank candidates independently within each category. The initial implementation can be deterministic/rules-based but must expose a versioned score and component signals so it can later be replaced with a learned ranking model.

Normalize numerical features to a comparable 0–1 range within each category. Suggested MVP score:

- 30% smoothed attach rate after a qualified impression.
- 25% incremental conversion uplift after exposure, when reliable.
- 15% gross-margin contribution.
- 15% contextual relevance.
- 10% fulfillment reliability.
- 5% freshness/exploration value.
- Apply explicit negative penalties for cancellations, refunds, substitutions, damage, and recent stock instability.

Contextual relevance can include:

- Primary bouquet/product attributes.
- Occasion and landing context.
- Recipient/customer intent where explicitly available.
- Selected city, delivery date, and time window.
- Seasonality and holidays.
- Price relationship to the primary bouquet.
- New versus returning customer.
- Existing cart composition.
- Device/session context where privacy-compliant.

Do not rank primarily by raw sales. Raw sales are exposure-biased and will reinforce already popular products. Use Bayesian smoothing or a minimum-impression threshold for attach and conversion rates. Reserve a configurable exploration percentage, initially approximately 5%, for eligible products with insufficient data. Log the exploration decision.

BACKEND / RECOMMENDATION SERVICE

Provide a single endpoint or service call for the module. Inputs should include, where available:

- Primary product and selected variant IDs.
- Storefront/country/currency.
- Delivery city/zone/date/time.
- Occasion/category context.
- Cart item IDs and quantities.
- Anonymous session or experiment assignment.
- Logged-in customer segment only where permitted.

Return one result per eligible category, preserving the required category order. Each result should include:

- Category key.
- Product ID and variant ID.
- Display name.
- Image URL and alt text.
- Current incremental price and currency.
- Availability status.
- Whether required options exist.
- Allowed quantity range.
- Ranking score or opaque score reference.
- Model/rules version.
- Recommendation/tracking token.
- Experiment ID and variant where relevant.
- Fallback reason if the top candidate was excluded.

If no eligible item exists for a category, omit that slot cleanly. Never show an out-of-stock or undeliverable recommendation merely to maintain four cards. Do not replace a missing category with a duplicate category unless product explicitly approves that fallback.

FRONTEND STATE AND “+ ADD” BEHAVIOR

Maintain selected add-ons in local component state until the customer presses the primary CTA.

For a simple product with no required choices:

1. Customer presses + Add.
2. Select the item optimistically in local state.
3. Apply a visible teal selected border and checkmark.
4. Change the button label to ✓ Added.
5. Update item count and total immediately.
6. Announce the update through an ARIA live region.
7. If quantity greater than one is supported, reveal quantity controls only after selection.
8. Pressing ✓ Added again removes the item and recalculates the summary.

For a product with required choices, such as cake flavor or size:

1. Pressing + Add opens a lightweight inline selector, popover, or bottom sheet.
2. Require the customer to choose every mandatory option.
3. Show price changes caused by the selected variant before confirmation.
4. Only mark the card as selected after a valid variant is chosen.
5. Never silently select a flavor, size, or personalization value unless product has explicitly defined a safe default.
6. Preserve the selection if the customer closes and reopens the selector during the same page session.

PRIMARY CTA / CART BEHAVIOR

- The primary CTA adds the bouquet plus all selected add-ons in one customer action.
- The CTA label must reflect the current item count.
- Disable the CTA while the request is running and show a loading state.
- Prevent duplicate submissions using an idempotency key.
- Revalidate price, inventory, delivery eligibility, and selected variants immediately before mutating the cart.
- Prefer an atomic cart operation. If atomic mutation is unavailable, implement an explicit failure strategy and never report success for an item that was not added.
- On success, open the cart drawer or cart confirmation state listing every added item and the correct total.
- Preserve the recommendation/tracking token on cart lines or associated analytics so purchases can be attributed.
- If an item becomes unavailable before submission, do not silently substitute it. Explain which item changed, remove or refresh that selection, update the total, and allow the customer to retry.
- If the customer already has an equivalent add-on in the cart, follow the SKU’s multiple-quantity rule and avoid unintended duplication.
- Persist selected add-ons while the customer remains on the PDP. It is not necessary to persist them across unrelated browsing sessions for the first release.

ANALYTICS EVENTS

Implement consistent event names and payloads. At minimum capture:

1. upsell_module_view
2. upsell_item_impression
3. upsell_add_click
4. upsell_option_open
5. upsell_option_selected
6. upsell_remove_click
7. upsell_quantity_change
8. upsell_bundle_add_attempt
9. upsell_bundle_add_success
10. upsell_bundle_add_failure
11. upsell_item_purchased
12. upsell_item_cancelled
13. upsell_item_refunded
14. upsell_item_substituted

Every applicable event should contain:

- Timestamp.
- Session/anonymous customer identifier.
- Primary product and variant IDs.
- Upsell product and variant IDs.
- Category and slot position.
- Recommendation/tracking token.
- Model/rules version.
- Experiment ID and variant.
- Eligibility/ranking score reference.
- Displayed price and currency.
- Selected delivery context.
- Quantity.
- Cart/order identifier when available.
- Failure or exclusion reason where relevant.

Define a qualified impression consistently, such as at least 50% of the card visible for one continuous second. Avoid counting server responses as impressions when the card was never rendered or seen.

METRICS AND GUARDRAILS

Primary metrics:

- Upsell attach rate per qualified module view.
- Incremental gross profit per module view.
- Average order value.
- Purchase conversion for each category and recommended SKU.

Guardrail metrics:

- Primary bouquet add-to-cart and purchase conversion.
- Cart abandonment.
- Page performance and layout shift.
- Add-to-cart errors.
- Cancellation, refund, substitution, damage, and late-delivery rates.
- Customer-support contacts related to upsells.

EXPERIMENTATION

- Feature-flag the module.
- Support control versus treatment assignment.
- Keep the initial category order fixed.
- Log the ranking version and experiment variant on every impression and downstream conversion event.
- After sufficient clean data, test category order separately rather than changing both category order and ranking logic in the same experiment.
- Roll out gradually by traffic percentage and delivery market.
- Provide a kill switch that restores the current behavior or hides the module without a deployment.

RESPONSIVE AND ACCESSIBILITY REQUIREMENTS

Desktop:

- Bouquet summary remains visually distinct.
- Add-on cards fit without feeling compressed.
- If horizontal scrolling is required, indicate overflow clearly and preserve keyboard navigation.

Mobile:

- Use a horizontal card carousel or compact stacked layout.
- Keep touch targets at least 44 × 44 px.
- Ensure the total and CTA remain visible without obscuring content.
- Do not create accidental horizontal page overflow.

Accessibility:

- All actions must be keyboard accessible.
- Selected state cannot rely on color alone.
- Images require meaningful alt text.
- Buttons require explicit accessible names.
- Use ARIA live announcements for additions, removals, quantity changes, and total updates.
- Keep focus inside and return focus correctly from required-option dialogs.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.

PERFORMANCE AND RELIABILITY

- Do not block primary PDP rendering on recommendation retrieval.
- Lazy-load the module and product imagery where appropriate.
- Reserve layout space to minimize cumulative layout shift.
- Define a recommendation timeout. On timeout or service failure, hide the module or render a safe merchandising fallback without blocking the bouquet purchase flow.
- Cache only where inventory, delivery eligibility, pricing, and personalization rules remain correct.
- Monitor endpoint latency, error rate, empty-result rate, add-to-cart failures, and stale-price incidents.

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

- The module heading reads “Complete your gift.”
- Supporting copy reads “Add a thoughtful extra to your bouquet.”
- The primary bouquet is displayed separately and cannot be removed from the module.
- Slots appear in the exact order Chocolate, Cake, Balloon, Stuffed animal.
- Each visible slot contains no more than one eligible product from its assigned category.
- Products are filtered for current stock and delivery eligibility before ranking.
- Product names and prices match the selected variants and active AED storefront.
- No ambiguous currency glyph is used.
- + Add selects a simple product without immediately mutating the cart.
- A selected card has a clear visual and accessible selected state.
- The item count and total update correctly after add, remove, option, and quantity changes.
- Required cake choices are collected before selection.
- The final CTA adds the bouquet and all selected upsells and cannot be double-submitted.
- Cart confirmation lists exactly the successfully added products.
- Stale price, stock, and delivery failures are handled explicitly without silent substitution.
- All specified analytics events fire once with correct category, slot, product, ranking, and experiment metadata.
- Module failure never blocks purchase of the primary bouquet.
- Desktop and mobile behavior passes visual QA.
- Keyboard, screen-reader, focus, contrast, and touch-target checks pass.
- The feature can be enabled, ramped, disabled, and reverted through configuration.

QA SCENARIOS

Test at minimum:

- No add-ons selected.
- One, several, and all add-ons selected.
- Add then remove.
- Quantity increment/decrement where supported.
- Cake with one required option.
- Cake with multiple required options.
- Category with no eligible inventory.
- Candidate becomes unavailable after rendering.
- Price changes after rendering.
- Delivery city/date changes after recommendations load.
- Cart already contains the same SKU.
- Recommendation service timeout or error.
- Cart mutation partial/total failure.
- Rapid repeated clicks and double-submit attempts.
- Slow network.
- Logged-in and guest checkout.
- Desktop, tablet, and common mobile breakpoints.
- Keyboard-only and screen-reader flows.
- RTL readiness if applicable to supported locales.
- Analytics deduplication and attribution through completed order.

DEFINITION OF DONE

- Product/design approval of final desktop and mobile behavior.
- Backend eligibility and ranking service implemented with versioned configuration.
- Frontend module implemented behind a feature flag.
- Cart validation and idempotent add flow verified.
- Analytics schema documented and validated in staging.
- Automated tests cover core recommendation, selection, cart, and failure behavior.
- Manual QA completes the scenarios above.
- Monitoring dashboard/alerts exist for recommendation and cart errors.
- Rollback/kill-switch procedure is documented.
- Production rollout plan and initial experiment configuration are approved.

OUT OF SCOPE FOR THE FIRST RELEASE

- Generative product imagery or generative product copy.
- Fully autonomous online learning without guardrails.
- Personalized pricing.
- Silent product substitution.
- More than one recommendation per category.
- Persisting unfinished selections across unrelated sessions.
