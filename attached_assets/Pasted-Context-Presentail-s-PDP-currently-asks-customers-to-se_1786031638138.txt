Context

Presentail’s PDP currently asks customers to select delivery on each product page. The UX needs to support two materially different situations:

1. The customer is starting a new delivery and must choose a delivery method/date.
2. The customer already has a delivery configured in the cart, so the PDP should inherit and summarize that delivery instead of asking again.

The redesign must also make delivery prices fully transparent, show arrival times in the recipient’s local timezone, and prevent delivery fees or settings from appearing to apply separately to every product.

Reference product pages

• Free-standard example: https://presentail.com/en-lb/beirut/product/serenity
• Paid-standard example: https://presentail.com/en-lb/beirut/product/plum-florals
• Scheduled-picker example: https://presentail.com/en-lb/tripoli/product/summer-daisy-garden

Objective

Create a consistent delivery-selection system for product pages that:

• Defaults to the lowest-cost eligible delivery method.
• Keeps Express Delivery visually first, while keeping Scheduled/Standard Delivery selected by default.
• Shows the customer the full delivery amount they will pay.
• Explains how the express price is calculated.
• Communicates arrival time using the delivery destination’s timezone.
• Reuses an existing cart delivery when appropriate.
• Treats delivery as a delivery-group/order setting, not a repeated per-product choice.
• Never silently changes the customer’s delivery method or adds a premium fee.

Core domain model

Introduce or consistently use the concept of a “delivery group.”

A delivery group contains items that share the same:
• Recipient/delivery destination
• Delivery method
• Delivery date or ETA
• Delivery time window
• Delivery fee
• Operational eligibility constraints

The PDP must derive its state from the applicable delivery group, not simply from whether the cart is empty.

State 1 — New delivery / no applicable cart delivery

Section heading

Use:
“WHEN SHOULD IT ARRIVE?”

Card order and default

• Express Delivery must always appear as the first card.
• Scheduled/Standard Delivery must appear as the second card.
• Scheduled/Standard Delivery must be selected by default whenever it is eligible.
• Express may be selected automatically only if it is the sole eligible method.
• Do not determine the default from API array order. Use stable method IDs/types and eligibility.
• Preserve an explicit customer choice during re-renders. Re-evaluate only when eligibility changes.

Incomplete scheduled selection

When Scheduled/Standard Delivery is selected but a date/time is still required:

• Show it as the active method.
• Clearly state “Choose a date and 2-hour window” or the appropriate market-specific window.
• Do not automatically open a blocking modal on page load.
• Clicking the selected card, date/time control, or incomplete Add to Cart action should open/focus the date/time picker.
• Do not submit an invalid cart without a delivery window.
• The customer must understand what is still required.

Free-standard pricing example

Express card:
• “Express Delivery”
• “Arrives in approximately 90 minutes”
• “Estimated by 11:30 AM Lebanon time” — time must be dynamic
• “$15 delivery”
• “Free standard + $15 express upgrade”

Scheduled card:
• “Scheduled Delivery”
• “Choose a date and 2-hour window”
• “Free”

Paid-standard pricing example

Express card:
• “Express Delivery”
• “Arrives in approximately 90 minutes”
• “Estimated by 1:30 PM Lebanon time” — time must be dynamic
• “$22 delivery”
• “$7 standard + $15 express upgrade”

Scheduled card:
• “Scheduled Delivery”
• “Choose a date and 2-hour window”
• “$7 delivery”

Pricing rules

• Always show the full delivery amount as the primary price.
• Never use “+$15” alone when the customer will actually pay $22 for delivery.
• Express delivery price = applicable base/standard delivery fee + configured express upgrade.
• Show the calculation beneath the express amount in muted secondary text.
• If standard delivery is free, show “Free,” not “$0.”
• If standard delivery is paid, show the full fee, e.g. “$7 delivery.”
• All amounts must come from configured backend pricing, not hard-coded copy.
• Apply the configured currency and formatting for the storefront.
• A delivery fee belongs to the delivery group, not to every line item.
• Switching methods repeatedly must not duplicate or leave behind a stale fee.
• Do not add an order-total component as part of this task.

Arrival-time rules

The customer may be shopping from the UAE, France, UK, or another country while sending to Lebanon. Clock times must therefore use the recipient/delivery destination timezone, never the shopper’s browser timezone.

For Express Delivery show both:

1. A timezone-independent relative ETA:
   “Arrives in approximately 90 minutes”

2. A concrete destination-local time:
   “Estimated by 1:30 PM Lebanon time”

Implementation requirements:

• Store/transport timestamps in UTC.
• Format the customer-facing time using the destination IANA timezone, e.g. Asia/Beirut.
• Use the delivery city/address to resolve the timezone.
• Do not rely on the browser’s timezone.
• Handle daylight-saving transitions using timezone data, not a fixed UTC offset.
• Label the clock time “Lebanon time”; do not use “Beirut time,” EET, or EEST.
• Use server-authoritative current time/SLA calculations rather than trusting the client clock.
• Keep PDP, cart, checkout, confirmation, tracking, email, and support/order views consistent.
• Localize 12/24-hour formatting according to storefront language/locale while preserving destination time.

State 2 — Existing cart delivery: Express

When an applicable cart delivery group already uses Express Delivery, do not show the two radio cards and do not ask the customer to choose again.

Replace the selector with one inherited-delivery summary card.

Heading:
“DELIVERY FOR THIS ORDER”

Summary content:

• Lightning icon
• “Express Delivery”
• Status pill: “Selected in cart”
• “Arrives in approximately 90 minutes”
• “Estimated by 1:30 PM Lebanon time” — dynamic
• Full delivery amount, e.g. “$22 delivery”
• Price breakdown, e.g. “$7 standard + $15 express upgrade”
• Fee status: “Already applied to this order”
• Explanatory copy:
  “This item will use the same delivery as the other item in your cart.”
• Action:
  “Change delivery”

UX requirements:

• The card should look like an applied cart setting, not an active radio choice.
• Use a subtle teal border/tint and no radio controls.
• Add/update the cart badge using the actual cart quantity.
• “Change delivery” expands or opens the full delivery selector.
• Before applying a change, explain:
  “Changing delivery will apply to all items in this delivery.”
• The customer must not be charged the delivery fee again when adding another eligible item.
• Use singular/plural copy based on existing item count.

State 3 — Existing cart delivery: Scheduled

When an applicable cart delivery group already has a scheduled date/time, collapse the full date/time picker into an inherited summary card.

Heading:
“DELIVERY FOR THIS ORDER”

Summary content example:

• Calendar icon
• “Scheduled Delivery”
• Status pill: “Selected in cart”
• “Today, 6 Aug”
• “6 PM–10 PM Lebanon time”
• “$29 delivery”
• “Already applied to this order”
• Explanatory copy:
  “This item will use the same delivery window as the other item in your cart.”
• Action:
  “Change date or time”

UX requirements:

• Do not display the full calendar/time-slot picker by default.
• Do not ask the customer to reselect the window.
• “Change date or time” expands/opens the picker with the existing selection prefilled.
• Before saving a change, state:
  “Changes will apply to all items in this delivery.”
• Preserve the chosen window if the customer closes/cancels editing.
• Use destination-local date/time and label “Lebanon time.”
• The delivery fee must clearly read as already applied, not as an additional fee for this product.

Cart threshold and repricing behavior

Adding another product may change standard-delivery eligibility or cross a free-delivery threshold.

Requirements:

• Recalculate delivery-group pricing using the prospective/new cart subtotal.
• If the threshold is crossed, update the base fee and express breakdown immediately after the cart mutation.
• Example: $7 standard + $15 express may become free standard + $15 express.
• Never charge the old and new fees simultaneously.
• Never present delivery as free if the backend will still charge a fee.
• Keep PDP, mini-cart, cart, checkout, and final order totals synchronized.
• If practical, preview a material fee change before Add to Cart; otherwise update it immediately and visibly after Add to Cart.
• Track the reason for repricing, e.g. threshold_crossed, location_changed, eligibility_changed.

Multiple recipients / multiple delivery groups

Do not inherit a delivery globally when the cart supports multiple recipients or destinations.

If the system can determine that the product belongs to an existing delivery group:
• Reuse that group and show the inherited summary.

If the destination/group is ambiguous:
• Ask the customer to choose:
  “Add to the current delivery”
  “Create a separate delivery”

Show enough context to distinguish groups:
• Recipient name when available
• Destination city
• Delivery method
• Date/window or Express ETA

Do not silently merge products for different recipients or delivery destinations.

Eligibility conflicts

Before inheriting an existing delivery, verify that the new product is eligible for:

• Destination
• Delivery method
• Scheduled date
• Time window
• Express SLA
• Quantity/stock
• Product preparation lead time
• Operational/vendor constraints

If the product is not eligible for the existing delivery:

• Do not silently change the current delivery.
• Do not allow an invalid Add to Cart.
• Show a clear explanation, e.g.:
  “This item can’t arrive with your current Express delivery.”
  or
  “This item isn’t available for your selected delivery window.”
• Offer the narrowest valid choices:
  1. Change delivery for the existing group/all applicable items.
  2. Create a separate delivery, if supported.
  3. Select another product.

Cart and backend integrity

• The delivery method/date/window visible on the PDP must match the payload sent to cart.
• The cart must use the applicable delivery-group ID rather than creating a duplicate group on each Add to Cart.
• Server-side validation must reject stale or ineligible methods/windows.
• Rehydrate the correct delivery state after refresh, sign-in, navigation back from cart, or session restoration.
• Preserve state across product variant and quantity changes unless eligibility changes.
• Avoid race conditions from multiple tabs or cart updates.
• If the server corrects stale state, explain the change to the customer rather than changing it silently.
• Delivery fees must be idempotent and applied once per delivery group.

Responsive requirements

Desktop:
• Keep summary/selection above Add to Cart.
• Maintain the current right-column hierarchy.

Mobile:
• Cards and summary must fit without horizontal scrolling.
• Keep the price and method readable without truncation.
• Allow secondary price breakdown to wrap cleanly.
• Keep Change actions and Add to Cart within an accessible tap area.

Accessibility

• Use proper radio-group semantics only in the editable selector.
• Do not use radio semantics for inherited summary cards.
• Ensure keyboard access for delivery cards, Change delivery, Change date/time, and the picker.
• Provide visible focus styles.
• Announce selected method, full delivery price, fee breakdown, date/window, destination timezone, and incomplete requirements to screen readers.
• Do not communicate selection using color alone.
• Status pills must remain readable at supported contrast ratios.
• Handle error/conflict messages with an accessible live region.
• Ensure touch targets meet minimum size requirements.

Localization

• Add all new strings to localization resources.
• Support English and Arabic/RTL.
• Do not concatenate translated fragments to create pricing breakdowns.
• Use locale-aware currency, date, and time formatting.
• Confirm layout with longer Arabic text.
• “Lebanon time” must be localized while retaining the destination-time meaning.

Acceptance criteria

1. With no applicable cart delivery and both methods eligible, Express appears first but Scheduled/Standard is selected by default.
2. Express is never preselected merely because it appears first.
3. When standard is free, its card displays “Free.”
4. When standard costs $7, its card displays “$7 delivery.”
5. Express displays the full fee, e.g. “$22 delivery,” not only “+$15.”
6. Express displays the base fee + upgrade breakdown.
7. Express shows both a relative ETA and a destination-local estimated time labeled “Lebanon time.”
8. Browser timezone does not change the delivery clock time.
9. An incomplete scheduled selection cannot submit an invalid Add to Cart.
10. An existing eligible Express cart delivery displays a single inherited summary, not radio cards.
11. An existing scheduled cart delivery displays the saved date/window summary, not the open picker.
12. Inherited delivery fees are labeled as already applied and are not charged again per product.
13. Change delivery/date actions warn that changes apply to all items in the delivery group.
14. Adding an item reuses the existing delivery-group ID when appropriate.
15. Crossing the free-delivery threshold recalculates the delivery fee and express breakdown exactly once.
16. An ineligible product shows a conflict and does not silently alter the cart.
17. Multiple-recipient carts do not inherit or merge an ambiguous delivery group.
18. PDP, mini-cart, cart, checkout, order payload, and confirmation show consistent delivery data.
19. State survives refresh, back navigation, and authenticated session restoration.
20. Desktop, mobile, English, Arabic/RTL, keyboard, and screen-reader flows pass QA.

Definition of done

• Product/UX behavior agreed with stakeholders.
• Frontend and backend delivery-group logic implemented.
• Delivery pricing is server-backed and idempotent.
• Automated unit tests cover method defaults, full-fee calculations, inheritance, threshold changes, and conflict handling.
• Integration tests cover PDP → cart → checkout for new and inherited deliveries.
• End-to-end tests cover Express, Scheduled, paid/free standard, and cart reuse.
• QA signs off on the full matrix in staging.
• Analytics events and payloads are validated in staging.
• English and Arabic translations are reviewed.
• Accessibility checks pass.
• Before/after screenshots or recordings for each key state are attached to this task.
• Release is feature-flagged if available.
• Rollout begins with Lebanon storefronts and is monitored before broader adoption.

Success metrics

Primary:
• PDP Add to Cart conversion
• Checkout completion
• Delivery-selector completion rate

Guardrails:
• Express upgrade rate and revenue
• Delivery-margin impact
• Delivery-fee calculation errors
• Duplicate delivery-fee incidents
• Delivery-method changes after Add to Cart
• Checkout abandonment after delivery review
• Customer-support contacts about unexpected fees, delivery timing, or repeated selection

Rollout monitoring

Monitor separately for:
• New delivery vs inherited delivery
• Free-standard vs paid-standard products
• Express vs Scheduled
• Domestic shoppers vs customers browsing from outside Lebanon
• Desktop vs mobile
• English vs Arabic

Use the data to confirm that the redesign improves comprehension and conversion without reducing valid Express upgrades or creating fulfillment errors.
